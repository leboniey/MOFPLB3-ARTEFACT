1. Population size: what makes a population change?

A population's size is the number of individuals in that population. Four processes can change it:

Natality = births added to the population.
Mortality = deaths removed from the population.
Immigration = individuals entering the population.
Emigration = individuals leaving the population.
Key idea: Natality + immigration increase population size. Mortality + emigration decrease population size.

2. Population growth and carrying capacity

A population may grow rapidly when conditions are favourable. As resources become limited, growth slows.

Carrying capacity (K) is the largest number of individuals of a species that an environment can support over a long period. Environmental resistance is the combined effect of factors that prevent unlimited population growth.

Two population growth patterns

Geometric growth produces a J-shaped curve when a population grows exponentially under favourable conditions.

Logistic growth produces an S-shaped curve because growth slows as environmental resistance increases and the population approaches carrying capacity.

Choose a phase to learn what is happening.

What the S-shape shows

  1. Lag phase: growth starts slowly.
  2. Exponential phase: population increases rapidly under favourable conditions.
  3. Stationary phase: growth slows as the population approaches carrying capacity.

6. Quadrat sampling: learn to estimate a plant population

A quadrat is a sample area used to study organisms that are fixed in one place, especially plants. We use several sample quadrats instead of counting the entire field.

How to calculate:
1. Select 3 different quadrats.
2. Record the number of plants in each quadrat.
3. Add the three counts together.
4. Divide by 3 to get the mean number of plants per 1 m².
5. Multiply the mean by the total area of the field to estimate the population.

7. Mark-recapture: learn the calculation

Mark-recapture is used to estimate a population when individuals are mobile and difficult to count completely.

Step 1: Choose how many fish you want in your first sample. Mark those fish.
Step 2: Release them so they mix with the population.
Step 3: Capture a second sample. You choose when to stop catching.
Step 4: Count how many fish in the second sample are marked.
Step 5: Calculate the estimated population.
Formula: Estimated population (N) = (n₁ × n₂) ÷ m₂
n₁ = number marked in first sample; n₂ = number caught in second sample; m₂ = marked fish caught again in second sample.
